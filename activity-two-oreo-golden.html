01 · The brief

Make Colombia take the first bite

OREO is a household name here. OREO Golden is not. Same two cookies, same cream, but vanilla instead of cocoa, golden on the outside and inside. Most Colombians do not know it exists, and far fewer have tried it.

You are a creative agency. OREO hired you to build an awareness and trial campaign for the Colombian market, aimed at Gen Z and young Millennials. The goal is not that people like the idea. The goal is that a real person, in a real tienda, actually buys one and tastes it.

The one sentence that matters

Everything you make gets judged against this: did it make somebody who had never tasted OREO Golden go out and taste it? A campaign that is beautiful and does not move that number is a campaign that failed.

02 · The product

Check the client's story before you believe it

The brief calls OREO Golden "a hidden gem waiting to be discovered" in Colombia. Your very first job is to find out whether that is true. We already looked, and the answer is more interesting than the brief suggests.

Same shape, same cream, same embossed pattern everybody recognises. The only thing that changed is the colour, and that is exactly why it is easy to walk past without noticing it exists.

What we found in five minutes

OREO Golden is already on sale in Colombia. It is listed in Mondelēz's own Colombian store, it is on Rappi, and Mondelēz has gone further and launched OREO Golden Fresa for this market.

So the barrier is not availability. It is that nobody knows it is there, or nobody has a reason to reach for it. That is a completely different problem, and it changes what your campaign has to do. Distribution problems are solved with logistics. Awareness problems are solved with ideas.

Verify it yourself, do not take our word for it: tiendamondelez.com.co · Rappi Colombia

Go and look at it in the real world

  • Walk into a tienda de barrio, a D1, an Ara and a big supermarket. Is it there? At what price? Next to what?
  • If it is not on the shelf, ask the shopkeeper why. That answer is data.
  • Buy one. You cannot sell a taste you have never tasted.
03 · Field work

Twenty interviews. No exceptions.

Every group interviews twenty real people in the target age range. Not friends who will tell you what you want to hear. Twenty people, and at least ten of them outside your own circle. This is the difference between a campaign built on evidence and a campaign built on a vibe.

Use the kit below. It has eight questions and every one of them earns its place: each answer feeds a specific box in your brief. Tap the answers as you interview, and the page keeps the running totals for you.

Two rules that make the data worth having

  • Do not explain the product first. If you describe OREO Golden before asking question 2, you have destroyed your own awareness measurement.
  • Write down what they say in their own words. The buttons give you the numbers. The sentence somebody says out loud is what becomes your campaign line.
04 · Market analysis

Your opinion is not analysis

The brief asks you to justify every choice with Colombian consumer behaviour, snacking trends, media habits and cultural flavour. That means numbers with a source next to them. Your twenty interviews are your primary data. These are your secondary sources.

Key Insight is not a summary

A summary says "most people don't know OREO Golden exists". An insight explains something true about the person that you can act on. Something closer to: "people don't try new OREO flavours because the classic never disappoints, so trying something else feels like risking a snack you already know is good." If your insight could have been written before doing the interviews, it is not an insight.

05 · The plan

What happens, in order

This is the whole activity laid out. Do it in this order. Every step produces something the next step needs, so skipping one leaves you writing a brief with nothing in it.

StepWhat happensWhat you walk away with
1Verify the client. Go to Rappi and to a real shelf. Is OREO Golden there, at what price, next to what? A photo of the shelf and a real price
2Taste it. Buy one and eat it as a team. Write down the first three words each of you says. Three words per person. Campaign lines hide there
3Field work. Twenty interviews with the kit in section 03. No explaining the product before question 2. Your awareness and trial baseline
4Desk research. Secondary sources on Colombian snacking and media habits, each one linked. Data with sources next to it
5Write the brief. The seven parts below, in order. Each one pulls from what you already gathered. The written brief, 3.0 points
6Name it. Only now. A name invented before the interviews is a name about you, not about them. The campaign name, 1.0 point
7Make the piece. Sixty seconds, vertical, sound off, hook in the first two. The video, 1.0 point

The seven parts of your brief, explained

These are the boxes you have to fill. Next to each one is where its content comes from, so nothing has to be invented.

PartWhat it actually meansComes from
Objective Awareness and trial. That is the objective, it is already given to you. Your job is not to invent a different one, it is to build a campaign that actually delivers those two things. Given in the brief
Target Audience Gen Z and young Millennials. Also given. Use your interviews to describe how these two actually behave in Colombia, because they do not behave the same way. Q1 and Q8

El brief es el resumen de lo que va a pasar

No es una lista de ideas sueltas. Es contar, en orden, qué va a pasar en la campaña y por qué. Mientras lo escribes, respóndete esto:

  • ¿Dónde los vas a contactar?
  • ¿Por qué TikTok o Instagram? ¿O vas a usar las dos, y para qué sirve cada una?
  • ¿Vas a hacer alguna actividad en tiendas, o dejas la prueba solamente en redes?
  • ¿Te imaginas eventos donde esta marca esté presente?
  • ¿Te tomarías la universidad con OREO Golden?
  • Si tú tampoco las conocías, ¿dónde te imaginas conociéndolas? ¿Consideraste eso en tus actividades de promoción?
  • ¿En qué momentos comes galletas?
  • ¿Te imaginas una malteada de OREO Golden? ¿O una colaboración gigante, tipo un McFlurry?

Si tu brief responde estas ocho, ya está escrito.

The activities you can choose from

These are the usual tools for an awareness and trial campaign. You do not need all of them. Two done properly beat six listed on a slide.

ActivityWhat it is and when it worksWhat it costs you
Sampling Physically putting the cookie in someone's hand. The only activity that guarantees trial rather than hoping for it. Strongest where your target already is: campus, gyms, transport hubs, tiendas near universities. Product, people and permission to be there
Retail activation
(trade marketing)		 Changing what happens at the shelf: a stand, a second placement next to the classic, a promoter. Catches people who are already buying snacks. Negotiating with the store
Street marketing An unexpected intervention in a public place, built to be filmed by the people who walk past. Earns reach without paying for it, if the idea is good enough. A genuinely good idea. Nothing else saves it
Creators and influencers Somebody the target already trusts tries it on camera. Micro creators in Colombia convert better than famous ones for a food first-taste. Fee or product, plus losing control of the message
Challenge or trend A repeatable action people copy. Works when it is easy to film, easy to explain and gives the person a reason to post themselves. Nothing, if it catches. Everything, if it does not
Paid social Buying reach on the platform Q8 told you they use. Good for reminding, weak for convincing somebody to taste something new on its own. Budget, and it stops the day you stop paying

Pick activities that talk to each other

A campaign is not a list. If your big idea is a challenge, sampling is what starts it and the creators are what spread it. If a judge cannot see how each activity feeds the next one, you built six campaigns instead of one.

06 · What you hand in

Three things, and they must agree with each other

DeliverableWhat it has to containPoints
Campaign name Original, catchy, made for Colombia. Fun, bold or challenge-based. It should make somebody want to taste it. Say it out loud before you commit. If it is hard to pronounce, it is dead.1.0
Campaign brief Objective · Target Audience · Market Analysis with data and the 6W · Key Insight · Activity · Chosen Channels · Schedule of your activities.3.0
Promotional piece One minute maximum. Reel, TikTok or YouTube Short. It must serve the trial goal, be visually fun, and be locally relatable. Acted out or storyboarded with narration both count.1.0

About the schedule, because this is where most groups lose points

A campaign is not a pile of good ideas, it is a sequence. Show week one, week two, week three: what happens, where, and who is doing it. If the sampling comes before anybody has heard of the product, you wasted the samples. If the creators post before there is anything to buy, you wasted the creators.

Be realistic about what a student team with a small budget can run in three weeks. A short plan you could actually execute reads far better than a three month plan nobody believes.

Sound off, thumb moving

Assume the person is scrolling with the sound muted and their thumb already moving. If your first two seconds need audio or explanation to make sense, you lost them before the cookie appeared.

08 · How you are graded

Five points, all of them Colombian

Everything here is graded against one filter: would this work in Colombia? Not in general, not in the United States, not in a case study you read. Here, with these people, in these stores, at these prices.

Escribe en el idioma que le hable a la gente

El nombre, los textos de la pieza y lo que dicen los personajes van en el idioma en que tu público realmente habla. Si la frase funciona mejor en español, va en español. Si el nombre pega mejor con una palabra en inglés que en Colombia ya se usa normal, úsala. Lo que no funciona es traducir una idea gringa al español y esperar que suene natural. El análisis y la parte técnica del brief pueden ir en inglés.

CriterionWhat gets the pointPoints
Campaign name It works out loud, in Colombian Spanish, said by a 20 year old. It survives being shouted across a tienda. It is not a translation of an English line, and it is not a pun that only works if you already speak English.1.0
Brief · analysis Colombian data with sources: how people here snack, where they buy, what they pay. The 5W answered, and your twenty interviews visible in the reasoning. Prices in pesos, channels that exist here.1.5
Brief · insight and idea An insight that could only have come from talking to Colombians, and a big idea that follows from it. If the same idea would run unchanged in Mexico or Spain, it is not adapted, it is generic.1.0
Brief · channels and schedule Channels justified by Q8 and by where your target actually buys: tienda de barrio, D1, Ara, Rappi, campus. Plus the schedule of your activities, in order and with dates.0.5
Promotional piece Under a minute, serves trial, and unmistakably made here. Colombian accent, Colombian places, Colombian humour. A viewer should know what country this is in within two seconds.1.0
