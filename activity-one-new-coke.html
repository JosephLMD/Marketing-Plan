01 · The brief

The brief

Coca-Cola has a platform called Creations: limited edition drinks built around an idea rather than a fruit. They want the next one. Your group is the agency.

You are not being asked to invent a soft drink. You are being asked to invent a reason for one to exist, and then make people care. That is the whole exercise. The flavour is the easy part.

Words you will need

Creation
A limited edition Coca-Cola built around a concept, with its own name, can and campaign.
Awareness
How many people know it exists. The first rung of the ladder, and usually the objective for a launch.
Big idea
The one thought the whole campaign hangs on. If you need three sentences, it is not one idea yet.
Key message
The single thing you want someone to remember a week later.
Key visual
The main image of the campaign. Yours has to be horizontal, 16:9.
Limited edition
On sale for a short window on purpose. Scarcity is part of the marketing, not an accident.
Work in groups, and make it fun. This is the one delivery where a silly idea taken seriously beats a serious idea done carefully. Coca-Cola sold a drink that tasted like a dream and another that tasted like pixels. Nobody on that team was being cautious.

02 · The platform

What Coca-Cola Creations is

Launched in February 2022 as a global innovation platform. Limited runs, unusual branding, aimed squarely at younger drinkers, and always tied to something digital.

The trick that makes it work: every Creation starts from an abstract idea, not a flavour. "What does space taste like?" is not a question a flavour lab can answer, which is exactly why it makes people talk. The drink is the answer to a question the marketing asked first.

Most of them are built on Coca-Cola Zero Sugar, run for a few weeks or months, and live through augmented reality, gaming, TikTok or a music collaboration rather than through television.

Creations is not the only platform doing this

Coca-Cola runs a second one built on culture rather than on product, and it is worth knowing about because it shows the same thinking taken somewhere else entirely.

Creations

Built on ideas

Limited edition drinks that answer a question: what does space taste like, what does a dream taste like. The drink is the souvenir of the idea.

since 202212 drops limited runs

Built on music

Started in Pakistan in 2008 and went global. Musicians record together and the sessions are released free. Coca-Cola is barely in the frame, and that is the point. It went global with a film called "The Conductor", where seven artists reimagine Queen's "A Kind of Magic".

since 2008 24M+ subscribers5bn+ views 180+ countries
Why this matters for your brief. Five billion views is awareness, and Coke Studio bought it by giving people something they actually wanted rather than by advertising at them. Notice also which direction it travelled: Creations goes global then adapts to each market, Coke Studio started in one country and grew outwards. Both work. Decide which way yours goes.

03 · The drops

The twelve that came before you

Read these the way an agency would: not "what did it taste like" but what was the bet. Red dots are the ones that changed what the platform could do.

February 2022
Starlight · the one that started it
"Space flavoured". A reddish cola meant to evoke stargazing round a campfire. Reviewers tasted cotton candy and vanilla. It also shows how Coca-Cola adapts a name by market: Starlight in the US, Intergalactic in the UK, Stardust in South Korea, Starshine in Thailand. Marketing Dive on the launch
April 2022
Byte · what pixels taste like
Gaming flavoured, "born in the metaverse", and sold only online. The bet was that a drink could launch inside a game rather than in a shop. Marketing Dive on the Fortnite launch
June 2022
Marshmello · the first artist
Strawberry and watermelon, with the DJ's name on the can. Extended with a Twitch mission and digital collectibles. The bet: borrow a fanbase. Marketing Dive on the collaboration
August 2022
Dreamworld · what a dream tastes like
Aimed openly at Gen Z and "the infinite potential of the mind". The most abstract prompt so far. Look at what the campaign actually was: an AR music experience with Tomorrowland, digital fashion with DressX, Snapchat lenses, bus takeovers, hand-painted murals, a Times Square billboard and pop-ups on campuses. That is the level of "promotional material" you are aiming at. Marketing Dive on Dreamworld
October 2022
Soul Blast · anime, and only in Asia
Launched with Bleach: Thousand-Year Blood War in Japan, China and Taiwan only. A Creation built for one region rather than the world.
February 2023
Move · with Rosalía
"Transformation flavoured", co-created with the Spanish artist, who also redrew the logo in her own hand for the can. The closest example to what a Latin American Creation could look like. Marketing Dive on Move
June 2023
Ultimate · with League of Legends
Made with Riot Games, sold in Turkey, China, the US, Egypt, Brazil and Canada. The flavour was described as tasting like +XP, which tells you the idea came before the recipe.
September 2023
Y3000 · co-created with AI
A cola imagining the year 3000, with the flavour and the artwork shaped with artificial intelligence, plus a fashion capsule with AMBUSH. The bet: the process itself is the story.
February 2024
Happy Tears · sold on TikTok Shop
Coke with a salty splash of minerals and a pinch of peach, launched for Random Acts of Kindness Day and sold only through TikTok Shop in the US and Great Britain. The channel was the campaign.
February 2024
K-Wave · K-pop in a can
Made with JYP Entertainment artists, built around the feeling of seeing your idols in person. Still running promotions years later, which is rare for a limited edition. Marketing Dive on K-Wave
May 2024
Wozzaah · made for Africa Day
Launched for Africa Day in Nigeria, Algeria, South Africa and Morocco. A Creation anchored to a date and a continent rather than to a celebrity.
September 2024
Oreo · the two-way collaboration
Two products at once: a Coca-Cola Oreo Zero Sugar, and an Oreo cookie with Coca-Cola syrup in one half and a red base embossed with the Coca-Cola script in the other, filled with creme holding popping candy so it fizzes. A frozen version went to McDonald's and 7-Eleven. The campaign was built on being "Besties": a Spotify feature that compares your music taste with a friend and builds a shared playlist, plus a "Bestie Mode" merch line with Forever21. Two brands, one idea, double the reach at no extra media cost. Worth copying. Food Dive on the Oreo partnership

04 · The patterns

Six things almost every Creation does

Take these as a checklist. If your idea does four of the six, it will feel like a real Creation.

1

Start from an idea, not a fruit

Space, dreams, pixels, transformation, the year 3000. Never "mango". The idea has to be something you cannot actually taste.

2

Borrow somebody's audience

An artist, a game, an anime, a K-pop label, another brand. You get their fans on day one instead of buying them.

3

Live somewhere digital

Augmented reality, Twitch, TikTok Shop, inside a game. The can is the souvenir; the experience is the campaign. Every Creation did this, and so will yours.

4

Make it run out

Limited on purpose. Scarcity is what turns a drink into an event and gets people posting before it disappears.

5

Bend it to the market

One drink, four names: Starlight, Intergalactic, Stardust, Starshine. Think about what yours is called in Colombia.

6

Change everything except the logo

New colour, new world, new story. The script stays. That is the thing you are borrowing equity from.

05 · The delivery

What you hand in

Four pieces. Five points. Everything below comes straight from the client brief.

1.0

Campaign name

The Coca-Cola name for your Creation, plus a slogan or two lines about the campaign.

  • It should sound like it belongs next to Starlight, Dreamworld and Y3000
  • Test it: could it be a Pepsi campaign? Then it is not there yet
1.0

The flavour

What it tastes like, and more importantly what idea the taste is answering.

  • Say the idea first, then the taste
  • "Tastes like the last day of school" beats "tastes like lemon"
1.0

The new design

The Creations key visual, horizontal 16:9. Exact sizes in section 06.

  • Show the can
  • The Coca-Cola script has to stay readable
2.0

The campaign brief

Six questions, answered in order. This is worth as much as the other three together.

  • Target: who?
  • Opportunity: why?
  • Objective: what? For this brief it is Awareness
  • Big idea: how?
  • Key message: what should they remember?
  • Schedule: when?
Happening in class

You are going to build your can in AR

On top of everything above, each group will put its Creation into augmented reality, so the can can be seen standing in the room rather than only on a slide.

You do not need to prepare anything for this now, and nothing about it is explained on this page on purpose. We do it together in class, step by step. Just know it is coming, and design your can properly, because it is the artwork from section 06 that gets used.

Your objective is Awareness

The brief asks you to write from the marketing objective. For this campaign it is already decided: Awareness. You are launching something that does not exist yet, so the job is to make people know about it and talk about it. Everything in your brief should be pointed at that.

Awareness→ Trial→ Purchase→ Repurchase→ Main brand
The main objective
Awareness. Not sales.

Nobody has tasted your drink and nobody has heard of it, so measuring purchases makes no sense yet. Judge your own ideas by how many people would find out and how much they would talk, not by how many cans they would buy. If an activity in your brief does not create awareness, cut it or explain why it is there.

The delivery template

  • Campaign name and a slogan, or a couple of lines about the campaign
  • Brief: around 500 words maximum, written from the marketing objective (Awareness), explaining the campaign activities
  • Promotional material: finished pieces, not descriptions. Ads, billboards, newspaper, TikTok, Instagram. At least two.
Finished means finished. "A TikTok where someone opens the can" is a sentence, not a piece of promotional material. Make the thing.

06 · Sizes

Design at these sizes

Guessing the size is how a good design ends up stretched or pixelated on the day. Use these.

PieceSize
Key visual, 16:9 1920 × 1080 px minimum. 3840 × 2160 px if it will be printed or projected. Both are exactly 16:9.
Can wrap, 330 ml 207 × 102 mm of artwork, plus 3 mm bleed on each side, so make the file 213 × 108 mm. At 300 dpi that is about 2449 × 1205 px.
Instagram post1080 × 1080 px square, or 1080 × 1350 px portrait
Instagram story, Reel, TikTok1080 × 1920 px, vertical 9:16
Billboard, horizontal Work to a ratio, not to pixels. 3:1 is common. Keep the message under seven words: people read it at speed.
Newspaper, half pageAbout 250 × 170 mm, at 300 dpi, in CMYK if it goes to print
Safe margin Keep text and the logo at least 5 % in from every edge, on every piece
Where the can measurement comes from. A 330 ml can is a cylinder about 66 mm across. The label has to go all the way round it, so the width of your artwork is the circumference: π × 66 = 207 mm. The can is 115 mm tall, but the top and bottom curve away, so only about 102 mm of height actually prints flat. If you design a beautiful can at 200 × 200 mm, it will not wrap.

07 · The rubric

How you are graded

Five points, split across the four deliverables. Read it before you start.

1.0
Campaign name
The Coca-Cola name for your Creation, with a slogan or a couple of lines. Judged on whether it is specific, memorable, and could only belong to your idea.
1.0
New design
The Creations key visual, horizontal 16:9, at the sizes in section 06. Judged on craft and on whether it carries the idea, not just decorates it.
1.0
Flavour
The flavour and the idea behind it. Judged on whether the taste answers a question worth asking.
2.0
Campaign brief
Target, opportunity, objective, big idea, key message and schedule, around 500 words, written from the Awareness objective. Plus at least two finished pieces of promotional material. This is worth double.
5.0
Total
Group work. The brief is the biggest single piece, so give it the most time, and write it after the idea is settled rather than before.
